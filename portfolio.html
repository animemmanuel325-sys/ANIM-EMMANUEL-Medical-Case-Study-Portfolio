CHART NO. 0042 — PORTFOLIO CASE STUDY

Fixing the back office a small practice doesn't have time to fix.

A self-directed training project simulating three of the most common bottlenecks in a solo or telehealth practice — scheduling, insurance verification, and patient intake — solved with the tools a remote Medical VA has on day one.

No live patient or client data was used at any stage. This is practice work built to be handed to a real clinic.

01PROBLEM

Three leaks, one back office

Small telehealth and solo-provider practices lose revenue and provider time to the same three gaps: scheduling conflicts and no-shows from manual booking, delayed or inaccurate insurance eligibility checks that push claims into denial, and disorganized intake records that slow down every visit. I set out to simulate solving all three with tools that need no new software purchase.

02APPROACH

What I did

01

Standardized patient intake in OpenEMR

Built a mock panel of 25 records on the OpenEMR demo platform and standardized intake fields — demographics, insurance ID, chief complaint, allergy flags — so any record could be found in under 10 seconds.

02

Rebuilt the booking system

Designed a shared Google Calendar with color-coded appointment types, buffer times, and automated reminder emails 24 hours ahead of each visit to cut down no-shows.

03

Ran insurance eligibility checks end to end

Cross-checked mock policy numbers against payer rules, caught 5 deliberately seeded errors — expired coverage, mismatched ID, wrong plan tier — and logged every correction in a tracking sheet.

04

Applied a HIPAA-aligned handling checklist

Wrote and followed a data-handling checklist — access logging, minimum necessary use, secure sharing — across every step, consistent with my HIPAA Compliance Training certification.

05

Documented it as a repeatable SOP

Wrote the whole workflow up as a Standard Operating Procedure so a practice could hand it to any new VA and get the same result on day one.
